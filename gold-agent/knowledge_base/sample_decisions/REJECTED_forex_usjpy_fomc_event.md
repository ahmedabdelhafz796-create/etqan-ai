## 4. Why Not Just Trade the Event?

### The "Trade the Event" Temptation

**Argument**: "USD/JPY is so bullish, even if FOMC is neutral/hawkish, it should stay bid."

**Counter-Arguments**:

1. **Forecast Accuracy**: Past FOMC surprises have reversed moves up to 3% in minutes
   - Example: March 2023 FOMC — expectations of pause, but hawkish, USD jumped 1.5%
   - Example: December 2023 FOMC — expectations of January cuts, but held firm, USD jumped 1.8%
   - Directional bias is fragile when news is this important

2. **Slippage Risk**: 
   - Normal USD/JPY spread: 2-3 pips
   - During FOMC event: 15-30 pips (or wider if volatility extreme)
   - Stop-loss at 149.00 could fill at 148.50 or worse (50+ pip slippage)
   - $100 planned loss could become $200+ loss in seconds

3. **Liquidity Risk**:
   - Volume dries up 10-15 minutes before announcement (algos pause)
   - Immediate post-announcement: flood of algorithmic orders
   - Manual traders (us) are at huge disadvantage vs. algo execution

4. **Opportunity Cost**:
   - If FOMC disappoints, lose 3-5% on position instantly
   - If FOMC neutral, trade reverses after 30 minutes (no net gain)
   - If FOMC bullish (only 1/3 probability), gain 1-2% = risk 3-5% to make 1-2%
   - **Risk/Reward is TERRIBLE** (1:0.5 at best case, -3% downside in worst case)

## 6. Professional Risk Management Rationale

### Why News Protocol Exists

**Historical Evidence**:

1. **Flash Crash (May 6, 2010)**: S&P 500 fell 9% in 5 minutes due to unexpected algorithmic selling during period of high uncertainty. Retail traders with limit orders were slaughtered.

2. **Switzerland Denies EUR Floor (January 15, 2015)**: Swiss National Bank shocked market by ending EUR/CHF floor. EUR/CHF crashed 30% in minutes. Retail traders with stops got ZERO execution (gap down past stops).

3. **Negative Rates Surprise (December 2014)**: ECB surprised with rate cuts + negative rates hint. EUR/USD crashed 3% in 10 minutes. Stop-losses created cascade of liquidations.

**Lesson**: News events create:
- Extreme volatility (normal spreads blow up)
- Gap risk (price jumps past stop-loss)
- Liquidity vacuum (no buyers at useful prices)
- Algorithmic predation (fast players scalp slow players)

**Professional Response**: Don't trade into news events. Wait for calm.
