# AAOIFI Sharia Standard No. 23 — Equity Screening

**Source**: AAOIFI (Accounting and Auditing Organization for Islamic Financial Institutions)  
**Standard**: Sharia Standard No. 23 — "Investment Funds"  
**Applicability**: Screening stocks and equities for Islamic investors  
**Last Updated**: 2023 AAOIFI Annual Review  

## Two-Layer Screening Methodology

Islamic equity screening applies two independent filters:

### Layer 1: Sector Exclusion (Hard Filter)

**Prohibited Sectors** (haram, impermissible business):
- Alcohol production, distribution, or retail
- Gambling, casinos, lotteries, gaming
- Conventional interest-based banking (interest/Riba)
- Conventional insurance (not Takaful)
- Pork production and processing
- Tobacco production (harmful substance)
- Weapons and defense contractors (military equipment)
- Adult entertainment (pornography, escort services)

**Rationale**: These sectors involve inherent Riba (interest), Gharar (uncertainty), or Haram (forbidden) goods. Layer 1 is binary: if company operates in ANY prohibited sector, it is REJECTED regardless of financial metrics.

### Layer 2: Financial Ratio Purification (Quantitative Filter)

Even companies in permitted sectors must pass ratio thresholds. Companies with excessive debt, interest income, or illiquid assets may violate Islamic principles despite operating in halal sectors.

#### Threshold 1: Debt-to-Market-Cap Ratio

**Rule**: Total Debt / Market Capitalization ≤ 33%

**Interpretation**:
- Debt exceeding 1/3 of market value indicates excessive leverage
- Islamic financing favors asset-backed, equity-based capital structures
- High debt ratios suggest Riba-dependent business model
- Compliance requirement: Debt must be ≤ 33% of market cap

**Source**: AAOIFI Sharia Standard No. 21 & 23; DJIM (Dow Jones Islamic Market) criteria

#### Threshold 2: Interest Income Ratio

**Rule**: Interest Income / Total Revenue ≤ 5%

**Interpretation**:
- Companies deriving >5% revenue from interest/Riba sources are non-compliant
- Interest income includes: bank interest on deposits, lending income, bond coupon earnings
- 5% threshold permits incidental interest (e.g., cash swept to money market fund) but rejects interest-dependent business models
- Compliance requirement: Interest income must be ≤ 5% of annual revenue

**Examples**:
- Company A: $100M revenue, $2M interest income = 2% (PASS)
- Company B: $100M revenue, $8M interest income = 8% (FAIL)
- Company C: $100M revenue, $4M interest income = 4% (PASS)

**Source**: AAOIFI Sharia Standard No. 23; MSCI Islamic Index methodology

#### Threshold 3: Illiquid Assets Ratio

**Rule**: Illiquid Assets / Total Assets ≤ 33%

**Interpretation**:
- Illiquid assets (goodwill, intangible assets, real estate for company use) should not dominate balance sheet
- Companies heavy in illiquid assets may hide true liquidity concerns (Gharar)
- Modern definition: Non-current assets minus tangible fixed assets
- Compliance requirement: Illiquid portion must be ≤ 33% of total assets

**Cash & Equivalents Requirement**:
- Minimum: 5% of current assets must be in liquid form (cash, marketable securities)
- Rationale: Ensures operational liquidity and transparency

**Source**: DJIM criteria; MSCI Islamic Standard

## Real Examples

### PASS Example: Microsoft (MSFT)

- **Sector**: Technology/Software (PERMITTED)
- **Debt/Market Cap**: $32B debt / $3.0T market cap = 1.1% (PASS, well below 33%)
- **Interest Income/Revenue**: $400M interest / $200B revenue = 0.2% (PASS, well below 5%)
- **Illiquid Assets/Total Assets**: Goodwill $80B, goodwill amortization handled via accounting (Goodwill represents acquisitions; modern AAOIFI guidance permits reasonable goodwill but limits excess). Asset analysis: $400B total assets, $80B illiquid (20%) (PASS)
- **Verdict**: SHARIA-COMPLIANT per AAOIFI Standard No. 23

### PASS Example: Apple (AAPL)

- **Sector**: Consumer Electronics/Retail (PERMITTED)
- **Debt/Market Cap**: $128B debt / $3.2T market cap = 4% (PASS, well below 33%)
- **Interest Income/Revenue**: $600M interest / $400B revenue = 0.15% (PASS, well below 5%)
- **Illiquid Assets/Total Assets**: Intangibles and goodwill $10B, total assets $350B, illiquid ~3% (PASS)
- **Verdict**: SHARIA-COMPLIANT per AAOIFI Standard No. 23

### FAIL Example: JPMorgan Chase (JPM)

- **Sector**: Banking (PROHIBITED)
- **Reason**: Banking sector is interest-based, inherently Riba-dependent
- **Verdict**: REJECTED at Layer 1 (sector exclusion), financial ratios not evaluated

### FAIL Example: Nike Inc. (NKE) — High Debt

- **Sector**: Apparel/Retail (PERMITTED)
- **Debt/Market Cap**: $9B debt / $110B market cap = 8.2% (PASS, below 33%)
- **Interest Income/Revenue**: $500M interest / $46B revenue = 1.1% (PASS, below 5%)
- **Illiquid Assets/Total Assets**: $5B goodwill, $40B total assets = 12.5% (PASS)
- **HYPOTHETICAL VARIANT**: If debt were $40B: $40B debt / $110B market cap = 36.4% (FAIL at debt threshold)
- **Verdict**: SHARIA-COMPLIANT under actual figures

## AAOIFI Guidance on Borderline Cases

### Goodwill and Intangible Assets

AAOIFI permits goodwill arising from acquisitions provided:
1. Underlying business is Sharia-compliant
2. Goodwill amortization is reasonable (5-10 years typical)
3. Goodwill does not exceed 25-30% of total assets

**Rationale**: Goodwill reflects premium paid for established business relationships (recognized in Islamic law if underlying business is permissible).

### Allowances for Banks as "Financial Services"

Some AAOIFI-compliant funds include:
- Islamic banks (Takaful-based, zero Riba)
- Financial services firms deriving <5% from interest

**NOT permitted**: Interest-based banking, conventional insurance, payday lenders.

## Implementation: Three-Step Process

1. **Sector Check**: If company operates in ANY prohibited sector → REJECT (Layer 1)
2. **Debt Check**: If Debt/Market Cap > 33% → REJECT (Layer 2)
3. **Revenue Check**: If Interest Income/Revenue > 5% → REJECT (Layer 2)
4. **Asset Check**: If Illiquid Assets/Total Assets > 33% OR Cash < 5% → REJECT (Layer 2)
5. **All Pass**: Approve for Sharia-compliant portfolio
